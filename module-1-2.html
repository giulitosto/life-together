Who I Am

⏱ ~50 minutes  ·  Individual

Before we can truly know another person, we need to know ourselves — not as a fixed thing, but as a story still being written. This section invites you to explore where you've come from, what experiences shaped you, and what you value most deeply. These are the roots your relationship grows from.

Do this alone, at your own pace. There are no right answers.

This is your space. You don't need to share anything here with your partner unless you choose to. The goal is honest self-knowledge — a foundation for everything that follows.

Steps

1

Set up your space

Find somewhere quiet and comfortable. Put your phone on Do Not Disturb. Make yourself a drink. This is time for you. (~5 min)
2

Your story so far — life chapters (~20 min)

Think of your life as a book with distinct chapters — periods defined by a place, a relationship, a role, or a turning point. Add as many chapters as feel right and reflect on each one.
  • What defined this chapter — what was happening around you?
  • What did you learn about yourself during this time?
  • What did it leave behind — a strength, a wound, a belief about the world?
3

What matters most — values (~20 min)

Below is a list of values. Start by clicking everything that resonates, even slightly. Then, from your selections, click again to mark your top 5 — the ones that feel most essentially you.
How it works: First click = resonates (dark). Second click = top 5 (warm). Third click = remove. Select as many as you like, but only 5 top values.
0 top values chosen  ·  max 5

Your top 5

For each value, write a sentence or two: why does this matter to you? Where in your life have you felt it most — or most missed it?
4

Patterns and threads (~10 min)

Look back at your chapters and your values together. What connections do you see?
  • Which values run through multiple chapters of your life?
  • Are there values you hold deeply that haven't had room to breathe?
  • What beliefs about yourself did you pick up along the way — and do they still serve you?
  • How do your values show up — or get challenged — in your relationship?
Your reflections
5

My Values Statement (~10 min)

Bring it all together. In your own words, write a short paragraph that captures who you are at your core — your story, your values, what drives you. Think of it as a personal compass: something to return to when you feel lost or face a difficult decision.

You might begin with: "I am someone who…" or "At my core, I am…"
