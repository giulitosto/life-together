Co-living

⏱ ~60 minutes  ·  Together
Video coming soon

A relationship doesn't just happen in the big moments — the trips, the milestones, the crises. It lives in the ordinary. The morning routine. The way you greet each other at the end of the day. Who makes the coffee. Whether you eat together. The small rituals that, accumulated over time, become the texture of a shared life.

This section is about designing that texture — deliberately and with care — across three dimensions: daily practices, shared space, and the cultivation of adventure.

Part 1 — Daily Practices

Small, consistent rituals carry enormous relational weight. A 30-second hug has measurable effects on stress and bonding. Saying goodbye with a real kiss changes the morning. A "how are you, really?" at the end of the day opens a different kind of conversation than silence.

Part 2 — Shared Space

The spaces we inhabit shape how we feel and how we relate. A home that's been thought about — that reflects both of you, that has places for quiet and for togetherness — holds the relationship differently than one that just accumulated.

  • Is there a space in your home that feels truly ours? What makes it feel that way?
  • Is there a space that feels neglected — that could become a place of connection?
  • Are there things in your shared space that don't belong to either of you — clutter, objects from a different chapter — that it might be time to release?

Part 3 — Adventure & Newness

Novelty is one of the most powerful forces in a long-term relationship. Couples who regularly do new things together — not necessarily dramatic things, just new things — report higher connection and satisfaction. Routine is necessary. But routine without newness slowly narrows the world.

  • When did you last do something together for the first time? How did it feel?
  • What's something you've each been wanting to try but haven't suggested yet?
  • What does adventure mean to each of you — it might be different?
