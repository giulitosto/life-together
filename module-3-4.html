Co-living

⏱ ~60 minutes  ·  Together
Video coming soon

A relationship doesn't just happen in the big moments, the trips, the milestones, the crises. It lives in the ordinary. The morning routine. The way you greet each other at the end of the day. Who makes the coffee. Whether you eat together. The small rituals that, accumulated over time, become the foundation of a shared life.

This section is about designing that foundation, deliberately and with care, sewn together across three dimensions: daily practices, shared space, and the cultivation of adventure.

Part 1 · Shared Practices

Small, consistent rituals carry enormous relational weight. A 30-second hug has measurable effects on stress and bonding. Saying goodbye with a real kiss changes the morning. A "tell me about your day?" over dinner opens a different kind of conversation.

Tick the practices you want to bring into your daily life, and choose how often for each. Add your own at the bottom.

Part 2 · Shared Space

The spaces we inhabit shape how we feel and how we relate. A home that reflects both of you has the capacity to hold the relationship in a state of resonance. That can be felt by yourselves and those who visit.

  • Is there a space in your home that feels truly ours? What makes it feel that way?
  • Is there a physical place in your space that could receive more love and attention?
  • Are there items in your physical environment that do not serve who you both are, or want to be today? Are you ready to thank them and let them go?

Part 3 · Shared Adventure

Novelty is one of the most powerful forces in a long-term relationship. Couples who regularly try new things together, not necessarily extravagant things, just new things, report higher connection and satisfaction. Routine is necessary; however, routine without newness can slowly narrow the world and therefore shorten the horizon.

  • When did you last do something together for the first time? How did it feel?
  • What's something you've each been wanting to try but haven't suggested yet?
  • What does adventure mean to each of you? Notice how it may be different.
