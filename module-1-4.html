I Am Enough

⏱ ~40 minutes  ·  Individual

The capacity to truly love another person begins with the capacity to love yourself — not perfectly, not without doubt, but honestly. This section has two parts: a self-compassion reflection, and an exploration of how you give and receive love.

Both are acts of self-knowledge. Both matter for what comes next.

Steps

1

Set up your space

This section benefits from stillness. Find a quiet spot, settle in, and take three slow breaths before you begin. (~3 min)
2

A letter to yourself (~15 min)

Think of someone you love deeply — a close friend, a sibling, your partner. Now imagine they came to you carrying the same doubts, the same self-criticism, the same tender wounds that you carry. What would you say to them?
Now write that — to yourself. Not to be read by anyone else. Not to be perfect. Just honest and kind.
  • What do you love about yourself — even if it's hard to say out loud?
  • What have you been through that deserves more credit than you give it?
  • Where do you need more gentleness from yourself?
  • What would it mean to be truly on your own side?
Dear me,
3

How you love — Love Languages (~15 min)

Gary Chapman's five love languages describe the ways people most naturally give and receive love. Most of us have a primary way of expressing love and a primary way of feeling it — and they're not always the same.
Rate how strongly each language resonates for you — both as a giver and as a receiver. 1 = rarely, 5 = this is deeply me.

4

My ways of loving (~10 min)

Bring together what you've noticed — your letter to yourself and your love language profile. Write a short reflection: how do you love, and how do you need to be loved?
  • How does the way you give love show up in your relationship?
  • Do you feel truly seen and loved in the way you need to be?
  • What might your partner not know about how you receive love best?
