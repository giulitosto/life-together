How I Am

⏱ ~40 minutes  ·  Individual

We all carry two sides. The parts of ourselves we show freely — our warmth, our wit, our resilience — and the parts we've learned to manage, hide, or haven't yet fully made peace with. Carl Jung called this second side the shadow: not the bad in us, but the unintegrated parts.

Knowing both your lights and your shadows is an act of courage and kindness — toward yourself, and toward the people you love. The goal here isn't to fix anything. It's simply to see yourself more clearly.

A note on shadows: Shadows aren't flaws to be ashamed of. They're often the flip side of a strength — your passion can become intensity, your care can become control, your independence can become distance. Naming them with compassion is the first step to working with them.

Steps

1

Set up your space

Find somewhere quiet. This section asks for honesty that can feel vulnerable — give yourself the conditions for it. (~5 min)
2

Map your lights and shadows (~25 min)

Add the qualities, strengths, and tendencies that feel true to you. Be generous with your lights — name what you genuinely bring. Be honest with your shadows — these aren't confessions, they're observations.
  • What qualities do people who love you see in you?
  • What patterns do you notice in yourself under stress or conflict?
  • What do you wish you could change — and what might it be protecting?

3

Look for the connections (~10 min)

Often a light and a shadow are two sides of the same coin. Look at your lists together.
  • Can you pair any lights with shadows? (e.g. passionate ↔ intense)
  • Which shadow do you find hardest to accept — and why?
  • Which of your lights has served you most in life?
Your reflections
4

Bring it into your relationships (~5 min)

Now look at your lights and shadows through the lens of your relationships, romantic, family, friendship, or professional.
  • Which of your lights do the people closest to you most benefit from?
  • Which of your shadows shows up most in your relationships?
  • What would it mean for the important people in your life to truly understand both sides of you?
