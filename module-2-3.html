Intentions & Words

⏱ ~45 minutes  ·  Together

The words we use in a relationship are not neutral. They shape what we notice, what we expect, and how we treat each other. Partner carries different weight than soulmate. We need to talk lands differently than can I share something with you?

Intention, too, is a force. A relationship held with clear, shared intention moves differently than one that drifts. This section is about getting deliberate — about the language you use and the intentions you set.

Read the theory

In systems coaching, we speak about the power of naming things — because what you name, you can work with. A relationship without named intentions is like a ship without a compass. It may still move, but not necessarily where you want to go.

Words, too, carry energy. Some words open space in a relationship — they signal safety, curiosity, care. Others close it down — they trigger defensiveness, distance, or resignation. Most couples develop their own language over time without ever noticing it.

Today you'll do two things: set shared intentions for your relationship going forward, and examine the words you use — naming what to bring more of and what to use less of.

Steps

1

Set your intentions (~20 min)

An intention is a conscious direction — not a goal with a deadline, but a quality you want to cultivate. Add intentions for your relationship: how you want to be together, what you're moving toward, what you're committing to.
  • How do we want to feel in this relationship — day to day?
  • What do we want more of? What are we moving away from?
  • What kind of partnership are we building?
Our shared intention for this relationship
2

The words between you (~20 min)

Think about the language you use with each other — the phrases that appear when you're stressed, when you're loving, when you're in conflict. Some words open things up. Some close them down.
  • Are there phrases one of you uses that land badly — and a better alternative?
  • Are there words or rituals of connection you want to use more?
  • What would you like to say more often? What would you like to say less?
How it works: For each entry, name a word or phrase to use less and the alternative to use more. This isn't about blame — it's about designing the language of your relationship together.
