Land Work

⏱ ~60 minutes  ·  Together
Video coming soon

Welcome to Module 2. You've each spent time getting to know yourself — your story, your values, your lights and shadows. Now you bring that self into contact with another.

Land work comes from the ORSC coaching tradition. The idea is simple: each person has their own inner world — their land — with its own landscape, rules, and tender spots. Before you can truly meet each other, you need to understand the territory you're each standing on.

Read the theory

Imagine your inner world as a piece of land — a landscape that is entirely your own. It has areas of beauty and ease, places that are still being tended, and edges where you post quiet signs that say handle with care.

Your partner has their own land. It looks different from yours. It has its own history, its own weather, its own sacred spots. Neither land is better or worse — they are simply different.

Most conflict in relationships happens when one person crosses into the other's land without realising it, or assumes the other's land looks just like theirs. Land work is about understanding each other's territory — so you can move between them with care and curiosity rather than collision.

Today you'll each map your own land and share it with each other. You'll also identify the shared territory between you — the space you're building together.

Do this together. Sit side by side or face to face. Each person fills in their own side. When you're both done, share — not to debate or fix, but to understand.

Steps

1

Set up your space

You're doing this together, so create a space that's comfortable for both of you. Phones away, drinks made, comfortable. Give yourselves permission to take your time. (~5 min)
2

Map your land — individually (~20 min)

Each person fills in their own column below. Think about what defines your inner world: your needs, your non-negotiables, the things that make you feel safe and the things that make you feel trespassed upon. Be honest. This is your land to describe.
  • What do you need to feel safe and settled in yourself?
  • What are your non-negotiables, the things you protect fiercely?
  • Where are your tender spots, places where you're easily hurt or overwhelmed?
  • What does someone need to know to move through your land well?
Be specific. "I need space" is hard to act on. "I need an hour alone after work before I can talk" gives your partner something real to work with.

3

Share your maps (~20 min)

Take turns, 10 minutes each, walking the other person through your land. The listener's role is to listen with an empathetic and caring heart, not to respond or fix. Listen as if you're visiting somewhere you've never been.
  • What surprised you about your partner's land?
  • What feels distinct or uniquely theirs about their land, separate from your own?
  • Is there anything in their land that helps you understand them more deeply?

4

The space between — your shared land (~15 min)

Beyond your individual lands, there is a third space — the land you share and are building together. What are the qualities you want that shared space to have?
  • What do you both need to feel at home in your shared space?
  • What rules or agreements would help you each feel safe there?
  • What kind of land are you building together?
